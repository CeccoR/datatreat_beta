UV-Vis DRS

Diffuse Reflectance Spectroscopy in the UV-Vis range. Used to assess light absorption of powdered samples. Semiconductor band gaps (Eg) can be inferred through Tauc Plot analysis.

XRPD

X-Ray Powder Diffraction. Diffractograms comparison, smoothing, and normalization.

GC

Gas Chromatography for H₂ and O₂ production rate assessment.

EPR

Electron Paramagnetic Resonance. Spectra comparison, smoothing and normalization.

SEM

Scanning Electron Microscopy. Tescan images read with their metadata (pixel size, magnification), cropped, normalised in intensity, grouped by scale and laid out into figures with scale bars.

Iodometry

Iodide to triiodide by H₂O₂, followed as absorbance against time: each trace aligned on its injection and read at a fixed time after it, as an absorbance or, calibrated, a concentration.

Custom

Plots of your own CSV and text files: columns as x, y and their errors, XY plots, bar charts and box plots, composed into figures.

Tabs in the background

A tab you leave keeps working, a refinement going on, and comes back at once. Left alone for the minutes set here, it sleeps: its state and its whole undo history are kept, and it wakes when you show it again, in about the time a project takes to open. A tab still working never sleeps. Any number of tabs can be open; beyond the tabs awake set here, the one you showed least lately sleeps first.

Groups and scale

The images are grouped by pixel size (within 10 % of each other), each group named by its magnification; a group's images share one scale bar in the figure. At common scale a group's images are brought to its largest pixel size (the others reduced by averaging, never enlarged) and to its smallest field of view, each crop narrowed about its middle (dashed in the crop editor). At own scale each image keeps its pixels and its crop, and gets its own bar. Click a group's name to rename it. A row of images wider than the box turns a page at a time with the arrows at its ends. Drag an image onto another group to move it there, or onto + Group to start a new one with it; + Group clicked adds an empty group, to drag images into. Reset makes the groups again from the pixel sizes. Each group makes its own figure: Figure composer lays its images out on a page in millimetres, as the plots' composer does, with their names, letters if wanted and the scale bars, for PNG at a chosen dpi or SVG.

Crop and intensity

Images. Every image, with its crop; a click shows it in the editor. The chain on a thumbnail links its image: linked images share one crop, narrowed to fit wherever an image is smaller (unlinked, an image keeps the crop it had). Link all links the images chosen in the selector, around the crop of the one in the editor. The selector chooses which images the intensity settings apply to: All, every image alike; a group, that group's images alone, the others keeping theirs. Going from All to the groups, each group starts from All's settings; going back to All, All takes the settings of the group on show. Crop. Each image starts with its default crop, the whole image less its info bar: Default ticked, its fields are locked; drag the frame's handles or sides to resize it, inside it to move it (which unticks Default). A ratio, when locked, holds while resizing. The toolbar on the image fits it, pans it, or zooms into an area (with a tool on, the wheel zooms and a double click fits). Intensity. Each image's grey levels go through one curve, drawn over the histogram of its crop: first the normalisation (none; a stretch of each image between two percentiles of its own levels; or the reference image's mean and SD, or its whole histogram, given to every image), then contrast, brightness and gamma; This image adds its own correction on top. Each Default locks its settings at their defaults. Statistics come from each crop, its saturated pixels left out. The images themselves are never changed.

Data

The arrows step through the files. Each column of the file shown has a role, set over it: x, y, err x, err y or — (not used); by default the first column is x and the others y. A line of series' titles over the header ("series 1", , , "series 2") sets the first roles instead: each title opens a group, its first column the x and the next ones, up to the next title, its y. As in Origin, a file may have several x: each y belongs to the x before it (none: its x is the row number), each err y to the y before it and each err x to the x before it, one error at most a column. A column set to err y where its y has one already takes that one's place, which becomes a y (so does an err x). The table, across the card, shows the whole file, scrolled inside its box; its first line names each series over its columns. Each y is a series, a trace of the trace list, "Series N (Cols x,y)" in its name's tooltip (its x's column and its own), named as its title names it (with the column's name when the title is over several y), else as the header names its column, else "Series N"; its name can be typed over in place, over its columns, or under Label in the trace list, one name in both (left empty, it goes back); its colour, picked in the trace list, or a palette given to all, is its colour in the plots; and the column's unit, in the header as "Name (unit)", "[unit]" or "/ unit", titles the plots' axes. The roles are each file's own; Same roles for all files gives those of the file shown to every file, column by column. + under this card adds a plot.

Analysis

Each trace is the absorbance of triiodide against time, the H₂O₂ oxidising the iodide; the small spike in it marks the injection of the analyte. The arrows step through the traces included. Click the plot to set the trace's injection at the point nearest in time (the guide over the plot shows that point); the results follow at once. On loading, and when ✦ Suggest is pressed (for every trace), the injection is the highest local maximum of the trace whose prominence is the minimum prominence at least (the prominence: how far the peak stands over the higher of the two valleys beside it); a trace with none has no injection until one is clicked. Each trace is then aligned: its time counted from the injection, its absorbance less the least it has from the injection on (its background, dashed). Its reading is that aligned absorbance at the reading time after the injection, linearly interpolated between the two points around it (none when the trace ends before). Calibration… turns the readings into concentrations: a line typed in (slope and intercept) or fitted by least squares to standards (concentration, absorbance), with its R², and the unit of the concentrations; it is kept in the project.
